Product vision

Decompose and orchestrate: the path to the payback AI promised.

Many companies don't find a return on AI — and the cause is rarely a missing bigger model. Our approach is to decompose agents and tie them into a workflow, working both levers of the return: cost and result. PlugHub was built for this.

Why AI started where it did

The architecture that was possible, given the constraint.

The first generation of AI in customer service was shaped by a real limit — short context, less capable models. Given those constraints, two strategies were the rational ones, and they worked: replacing point systems and platforms (a bot in place of the IVR, a classifier, a summarizer) and narrow-scope specialist agents. The more focused the problem, the better the result — because it fit the window and the model didn't get lost. It wasn't a mistake to fix; it was the architecture that was possible. But the constraint that justified it has loosened.

Why a bigger model isn't enough

A bigger context is necessary — not sufficient.

The temptation is to throw the whole process into a giant context and let a model resolve everything. Three things haven't changed, and they sink that idea:

Large context degrades

Dilution and lost focus in the middle of a huge context. A smaller, specific context is more accurate and more stable.

Long cycles are state, not size

A process spanning days, crossing sessions and waiting on third parties, fits in no context. It needs persistence and resumption, not more tokens.

Cost

A maxed-out context is reprocessed in full on every call — you pay for the whole even when the step uses a fraction.

The leap

Decompose agents + workflow.

It's applying to the process the strategy engineering has always used against complexity — decompose — and coordinating the parts: an orchestrator that doesn't know everything but calls whoever knows each step; reused specialists (human and AI, your own and external, including the ones already built); and a workflow with persistent state that stitches the steps together over time, surviving waits, drops and channel switches. This moves both levers of the return at once:

Cost falls by design

Each step carries only the context it uses, without re-sending the whole history every turn. It uses the right model — cheap for the trivial, expensive only where there's real reasoning. And steps that don't even need AI (a system call, a rule) pay no inference.

The gain rises — decomposing is the moment to redesign

Much of the return disappears when you automate the human process as-is — made of double-checks, queues and serial approvals that exist because of human limits. When you decompose, each step becomes visible enough for the right question: does this still need to exist? The gain is in the process that got shorter.

Redesigning doesn't conflict with reusing. The redesign happens in the composition — the workflow that reorders or removes steps — while the specialists stay reusable building blocks, recomposed in a new order. That's why decomposing makes redesign cheap. And the few blocks that don't survive the cut were the ones that only existed to compensate for a human limitation — not lost reuse.

The result

Lower cost, bigger gain, payback.

The return improves from both ends: decomposing tightens cost (fewer tokens, the right model per step, inference only where it adds value) and raises the result — the value of the whole process, redesigned, not of the isolated answer. And because each step is specific, it's measurable and auditable: the trust that finally lets you hand a real process to automation.

That's where the human finds their precise place — as one more specialist, who starts in command and steps back at the speed the evidence allows:

STEP 1

Human in command

The process starts led by people, with AI supporting where it's already safe.

STEP 2

AI takes what's proven

Each step that evaluation shows to be reliable is handed to automation.

STEP 3

Measured, reversible step-back

The human recedes at the pace the evidence allows — and takes back over instantly if trust drops.

The payback AI promised doesn't come from a bigger brain — it comes from the process decomposed, orchestrated and redesigned. Exactly what PlugHub was built for.
