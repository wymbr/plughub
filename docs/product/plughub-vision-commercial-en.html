Product vision

Decompose and orchestrate: the path to the payback AI promised.

Many companies don't find a return on AI — and the cause is rarely a missing bigger model. Our approach is to decompose agents and tie them into a workflow, working both levers of the return: cost and result.

Human + AI orchestration Long-cycle workflow Auditable quality No lock-in

How AI was applied to customer service always answered the context and model limits of the time. Those limits have changed — and the next leap in value doesn't come from a bigger model, but from decomposing long-cycle processes into orchestrated specialists. PlugHub was built for this.

1.Why AI started where it did

The first generation of AI in customer service was shaped by a real constraint — short context, less capable models. Given those, two strategies were the rational ones, and they worked: replacing point systems and platforms (a bot in place of the IVR, a classifier, a summarizer) and narrow-scope specialist agents. The more focused the problem, the better the result — because it fit the window and the model didn't get lost. It wasn't a mistake; it was the architecture that was possible. But the constraint that justified it has loosened.

2.Why a bigger model isn't enough

The temptation is to throw the whole process into a giant context and let a model resolve everything. Three things haven't changed, and they sink that idea:

Large context degrades

Dilution and lost focus in the middle of a huge context. A smaller, specific context is more accurate and stable.

Long cycles are state

A process spanning days, crossing sessions and waiting on third parties, fits in no context. It needs persistence, not more tokens.

Cost

A maxed-out context is reprocessed in full on every call — you pay for the whole even when the step uses a fraction.

A bigger context is necessary, not sufficient: it enables the next step, but it isn't the next step.

3.The leap: decompose agents + workflow

It's applying to the process the strategy engineering has always used against complexity — decompose — and coordinating the parts: an orchestrator that calls whoever knows each step; reused specialists (human and AI, your own and external); and a workflow with persistent state that stitches the steps together over time. This moves both levers of the return at once:

Cost falls by design

Each step carries only the context it uses, with the right model — cheap for the trivial, expensive only for reasoning. Steps that don't need AI (a system call, a rule) pay no inference.

The gain rises — decomposing is redesigning

Much of the return disappears when you automate the human process as-is. When you decompose, each step becomes visible enough for the right question: does this still need to exist? The gain is in the process that got shorter.

Redesigning doesn't conflict with reusing. The redesign happens in the composition — the workflow that reorders or removes steps — while the specialists stay reusable building blocks, recomposed in a new order. That's why decomposing makes redesign cheap. And the few blocks that don't survive the cut were the ones that only existed to compensate for a human limitation — not lost reuse.

4.PlugHub: the platform where the process runs end to end

Not just an orchestrator — orchestrating stitches the steps together, and the platform gives them everything else needed to become real operation:

  • Orchestrator + specialists, human and AI — equal participants in the same conversation; what makes gradual automation possible.
  • Long-cycle workflow with memory — steps that persist for days with suspend and resume; each reads only the context relevant to it.
  • The right model per step, no lock-in — swap provider by configuration; bring the agent you already have, including external ones.
  • The operation around it, native — channels (voice, WhatsApp, webchat, WebRTC, email), quality and compliance (masking, audit) on the same platform: the decomposed process becomes operable, measurable and auditable.

5.The result: lower cost, bigger gain, payback

The return improves from both ends: decomposing tightens cost (fewer tokens, the right model per step, inference only where it adds value) and raises the result — the value of the whole process, redesigned, not of the isolated answer. And because each step is specific, it's measurable and auditable: the trust that finally lets you hand a real process to automation. That's where the human finds their place — as one more specialist, who starts in command and steps back at the speed the evidence allows:

STEP 1

Human in command

The process starts led by people, with AI supporting where it's already safe.

STEP 2

AI takes what's proven

Each step that evaluation shows to be reliable is handed to automation.

STEP 3

Measured, reversible step-back

The human recedes at the pace the evidence allows — and takes back over if trust drops.

The payback AI promised doesn't come from a bigger brain — it comes from the process decomposed, orchestrated and redesigned. Exactly what PlugHub was built for.
