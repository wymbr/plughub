Product vision

Decompose and orchestrate: unlocking the true value of AI.

Most companies still aren't seeing real returns from AI — and a bigger model rarely solves it. The answer is to break work into specialized agents and connect them through a workflow, pulling both levers of ROI at once: lower cost and greater impact.

Human + AI orchestration Long-cycle workflow Auditable quality No lock-in

Every wave of AI in customer operations has been shaped by the limits of its models and their context windows. Those limits have eased — but the next leap in value won't come from a larger model. It comes from breaking long-running processes into specialized agents and orchestrating them. That's what PlugHub was designed to do.

1.Why AI started where it did

The first generation of AI in customer service was built around a hard constraint: limited context and less capable models. Two approaches made sense, and both delivered — using AI to replace individual tools (a bot instead of an IVR, a classifier, a summarizer) and building narrowly scoped specialist agents. The more focused the task, the better the results, because it stayed within the model's reach. That wasn't a misstep; it was the right architecture for its limits. Those limits have now lifted.

2.Why a bigger model isn't enough

The obvious temptation is to load an entire process into one enormous context and let the model handle it. But three things haven't changed, and each one undermines that idea:

Large contexts degrade

Detail gets lost inside an oversized context. A smaller, focused one is consistently more accurate and reliable.

Long cycles are about state, not size

A process that spans days, moves across sessions and waits on other people won't fit in any context. It needs to persist and resume — not more tokens.

Cost

A full context is reprocessed on every call, so you pay for all of it even when a step uses a fraction.

A larger context is necessary, but not sufficient: it enables the next step; it isn't the step itself.

3.The shift: decompose agents + workflow

Apply to your processes what engineering has always done with complexity — break them down — and then coordinate the pieces: an orchestrator that calls on whichever specialist owns each step; specialists you can reuse (human and AI, in-house and third-party); and a workflow that holds state and connects the steps over time. This moves both levers of ROI at once:

Cost drops by design

Each step carries only the context it needs and runs on the right model — inexpensive for routine work, costly only where genuine reasoning is required. Steps that need no AI at all, like a system call or a rule, incur no inference cost.

Impact grows — because decomposing is a chance to redesign

Most of the value is lost when you automate an existing human process unchanged. Break it apart and every step becomes clear enough to ask the real question: does this still need to exist? The gain is in the process you make shorter.

Redesigning doesn't conflict with reuse. The redesign happens in the composition — the workflow that reorders or removes steps — while the specialists remain reusable building blocks, recombined in a new sequence. That's precisely why decomposition makes redesign inexpensive. And the few blocks that don't make the cut were only there to work around a human limitation in the first place.

4.PlugHub: the platform that runs the whole process

It's more than an orchestrator. Orchestration connects the steps; the platform gives them everything else they need to run in production:

  • Orchestrator and specialists, human and AI — equal participants in a single conversation, which is what makes automation gradual.
  • Long-cycle workflows with memory — steps that persist for days through suspend and resume, each reading only what's relevant to it.
  • The right model for every step, with no lock-in — switch providers through configuration, and bring the agents you've already built, third-party ones included.
  • The surrounding operation, built in — channels (WhatsApp, webchat, SMS, email), quality measurement and compliance (masking, audit) on one platform, keeping the decomposed process operable, measurable and auditable. ⚠️ Voice and WebRTC: under rebuild, not delivered (measured 2026-08-19).

5.The outcome: lower cost, greater impact, real return

Returns improve from both directions: decomposition reduces cost (fewer tokens, the right model per step, inference only where it adds value) and increases impact — the value of the entire redesigned process, not of a single answer. And because every step is specific, it's measurable and auditable: the confidence you need to finally trust automation with a real process. This is where people fit best — as one more specialist who begins in control and steps back only as fast as the evidence allows:

STEP 1

People in control

The process begins led by people, with AI assisting wherever it's already proven safe.

STEP 2

AI takes on what's proven

Each step that evaluation confirms as reliable moves over to automation.

STEP 3

A measured, reversible handover

People step back only as fast as the evidence allows — and step right back in if confidence drops.
